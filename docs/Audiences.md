# Target Audiences for the Kanach Yerevan Website

This document outlines the primary target audiences specifically for the Kanach Yerevan website. We selected these audiences to help us achieve our mission of improving urban greenery through systemic change. While these groups overlap with the movement's broader supporters, this analysis focuses on how they use the internet, what information they need, and how the website helps engage them.

## 1. Community Advocates

These are our core supporters, often called watchdogs. They include local activists, engaged neighbors, and users of our digital tools like the Trees of Yerevan app. They are already motivated to protect specific trees or parks and want to stop illegal actions immediately. To support them, the website must serve as a source of solid arguments. They need actionable tools like clear "Good/Bad" verdicts on maintenance practices, report templates, hotline numbers, and calendar events. They also need validation through success stories to know that their efforts lead to real results.

## 2. Policy Makers

This group has the power to change how the system works. It includes the Mayor, the Council of Elders, Heads of Green Districts, and budget approvers. These officials are primarily concerned with what voters think, budgets, and avoiding legal trouble. Their goal is to fix city problems efficiently without losing support. Therefore, the website should provide them with draft plans and ways to reduce risks. They need proof of public demand, professional and calm analysis that explains why spending money is good, and ready-made rules they can adopt to improve the city.

## 3. Practitioners

This group consists of the professionals who physically work with the urban forest, such as municipal workers (HOC), private developers, architects, construction companies, and landscape contractors. Their goal is to finish their work efficiently, legally, and without people getting angry at them. The website must act as a technical guide for them. They need clear instructions on practical tasks like mulching and protecting roots during construction. They also need easy access to laws and lists of sellers for healthy saplings.

## 4. Residents

This large group includes families, students, and everyone who cares about heat, dust, and comfort in Yerevan. We call them Residents to include everyone. Their happiness or unhappiness is the power that changes politics. The website needs to show how trees directly affect their comfort and safety. By providing simple answers to daily questions and teaching them about quality of life, we can help turn their daily needs into requests for government action.

## 5. Experts & Media

Reporters, local biologists, international experts, and researchers act as independent judges of our work. Their involvement gives us the trust we need to make Policy Makers listen. Their goal is to find the truth and report accurate stories. The website must support them with checked facts, giving access to raw data, statistics, maps, expert quotes, and a full history of the movement.

## Content Strategy Note: The Dual-Layer Approach

For content regarding standards, such as leaf management or pruning, we use a layered approach to help multiple audiences at the same time. The top layer offers a visual verdict (e.g., Red or Green status) for Advocates to share immediately. The middle layer provides a plain English explanation to educate Residents. The deepest layer details the technical and legal rules needed by Practitioners and Policy Makers.
