This page is your guide to selecting trees and woody plants for planting in Yerevan, Armenia.
It combines species suited to the city's long, hot and dry summers, alkaline soils, and winter lows that regularly reach −10 to −15 °C with woody species recorded growing in Yerevan.
The list includes native trees of the Armenian highland alongside species long proven in the city's parks, streets, and courtyards.

The H and W columns give the average height and width of a mature specimen, in metres.
These are typical landscape dimensions rather than record sizes: a tree grown in a street pit or a dry courtyard will usually stay smaller, while one in an irrigated park may exceed them.
For climbers, H is the height the plant typically reaches on a support and W is its spread along it.

The OPALS column gives the [Ogren Plant Allergy Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogren_Plant_Allergy_Scale) rating for each species, from 1 to 10.
A score of 1 means the lowest potential to cause allergic reactions, and 10 means the highest.
A dash means no published score was found.
Scores are compiled from [published OPALS ratings by Thomas Leo Ogren](https://archive.org/details/allergyfreegarde00thom/).

## Reading the scores

OPALS rates the plant as a whole, including pollen, contact allergens, and strong odours.
Where a species has separate male and female trees, the male score is shown when no overall score exists, because male trees are the ones that release pollen.
Scores are species-level where published; otherwise the closest published species or genus rating is used. Scores can also vary between cultivars and between editions of the scale.

Many species are shown with a dash because no published OPALS rating could be found for them.
They remain on the list because they are useful climate-adapted plants, but they should not be treated as low-allergy choices without further local evidence.

A few species need careful placement for other reasons. Poplars and willows grow fast but demand water and regular structural pruning, while fig, pomegranate, and almond perform best on sheltered sites with reflected heat. Oriental spruce and European yew belong in irrigated parks and shaded courtyards rather than exposed street pits.

Tree of heaven is included because it is widespread on Yerevan streets, but it should not be planted: its invasive spread and allelopathic roots outweigh its tolerance of the climate.
